# Doodle Defense

A free test page for **Doodle Defense**, an original drawing game.

Play it here: https://doodle-defense-color-mixer.chirowsan.chatgpt.site/

The game fills the screen like a lined notebook page. Pick a crayon and a drawing tool, then draw a path with your finger. Friendly doodles hatch on the line and walk along it. Draw a loop and they circle as guards. Crossing colors makes a special spot: blue × red is a splash burst, green × blue is a bouncy bush, and red × green is a thorn patch. A triangle becomes a launch pad, and a loop around a star grabs it. Splashes fire on contact and recharge. Wet monsters briefly resist repeat splashes, so space your intersections along a trail. Red doodles hit hardest, but crowding one target gives diminishing returns.

## Color Mixer

**Rally Pin** opens on Page 3 and in Endless Sketch. Cross red lines to connect patrol routes, then select Rally and tap a red line. For 4 ink, the closest three connected defenders walk along those lines to patrol the pin. Move the pin to redeploy them, or select Rally and tap the existing pin again to clear it for free. There is an animated example in Guide → Combos.

The starter crayons are **red, blue, and green**. First-time clears earn **5 Mixing Ink per page and 20 per boss**. Finish Chapter 1 to earn 45 Mixing Ink and open the mixer. Choose which recipe to buy first. Each costs 45 and unlocks its crayon for the whole game. The next two chapter clears fund the rest. Replays don't grant duplicate ink, and the ink used to draw on a page is a separate budget.

- **Red + Blue → Purple Blink**. Swap enemies between the two ends of a line.
- **Red + Green → Yellow Zap**. Shock ground enemies, including armored ones.
- **Blue + Green → Teal Frost**. Slow Flyers, reveal hidden enemies, and stop Hoppers from jumping while on the line.

Crossing drawn lines still makes the original battle combos. Any Zap and Blink crayons unlocked in earlier versions stay unlocked.

**Drawing tools** cost different amounts of ink: the **Pen** is cheap, thin, and precise but weaker; the **Brush** is thick, sturdy, and more expensive; the **Highlighter** covers a wide area and can slow or reveal monsters, but it gets chewed through quickly.

Each crayon is labeled so you can see what it does: **Blue** slows monsters, **Red** tickles them, **Green** is a wall, **Zap** shocks, **Blink** swaps ends, **Frost** chills flyers and stops hoppers from launching, and the eraser rubs a line out. The first time you meet a color, tool, combo, monster, or hazard, a short pop-up explains it. Open **How to play** from the menu or the pause screen for the full list, including counters. Before every wave, the **scouting preview** names the enemy types but keeps entry points and routes hidden. Each wave rolls different approaches. Once it starts, red **!** marks give about three seconds of warning before a pack enters. Drawing costs less during a warning, so keep ink in reserve. A red loop around the house hatches extra guards with longer reach.

The **Bestiary** under How to play matches every troop and boss to its battle portrait. The portraits use pencil lines and lightly colored fills. Learn the shapes first: Armored has horns and a steel visor, the tiny orange Zipper is a lightning bolt, and Cover-bug carries a tall gold shield. Drop-fakers still pass as ink until revealed.

Green walls block monsters. If there's a gap they walk around; otherwise they chew through the wall (it cracks, then snaps). Armored monsters and bosses break walls faster. Hoppers jump over them.

Before each page you pick a few **seed packets** (3 on early pages, 4 later). Place them on the page: an **Inkwell** slowly fills during a wave and drops ink you tap to collect (drops fade if you leave them). Other packets unlock as you go: a **Decoy** that monsters rush to chew, a **Sharpener** turret, a **Glue pad**, a **Bumper**, and a **Reservoir** that holds more ink. After you place one during a wave, it recharges before you can place it again. Monsters chew your buildings too, so where you put them matters. Crayon lines help: blue waters an Inkwell, red sharpens a Sharpener, and green fences any part from chewing.

You can also **design your own defenders** from the menu or the loadout screen. Pick a color, face, size, a job (Poke, Slow, Mend, Gather, or Guard), and up to two traits, then spend ink to send them along a drawn line. They patrol back and forth. You can save three designs on your device.

Each page has a tight **ink budget** that barely refills, and you start with only part of it, so you can't draw a full defense right away. Place an **Inkwell** early and tap the drops to build up; collecting a star also refunds 20 ink. Drawing mid-wave costs extra, so every line is a choice. There are three worlds with five pages plus a boss in each, and an Endless Sketch mode. Mixed types show up from the early pages, so one color combo won't solve every wave. Some scribbles hop over vines, nibble lines, split in two, hide until they touch blue, fly over lines, wear armor, or heal others. Newer types include **Diggers** that tunnel under lines, **Ink-nibs** that steal drops, **Blotlings** that leave no-draw blots, **Cover-bugs** that shield others, **Zippers** that swarm, and **Drop-fakers** that pretend to be ink drops. Starting on Vine Fence, **Waxbacks** shrug off blue and splashes. Funnel them into thorns or use a Sharpener. **Flankers** spot green walls ahead and look for a gap instead of chewing when they can go around. A slow line or a trap at the opening can turn their detour against them. Later waves can send simultaneous attacks from different sides or keep healers close to their escorts. Launch pads aim for flyers first. Bosses change tactics as they get hurt. Finish a page for up to three stars (clear it, let nothing reach the house, and keep 35% of your ink after using a combo or launch pad).

**First Scribble** starts with red selected and shows where to draw your first line. The practice waves are smaller and slower, and drawing during them has no extra ink cost. Once you clear the page, the drawing guides and automatic tips stay out of the way, even on replays. The How to Play reference stays available.

**Loopy Guard** introduces a second approach in the later waves. Familiar monsters are only a little stronger than in First Scribble. You can pause and use **Restart level** to clear the page and start at Wave 1 with the same loadout and saved progress.

Defeated monsters refill your drawing ink automatically, up to the page limit. On Normal, most monsters give 2 ink, tiny monsters give 1, and bosses give 8. This is separate from the Mixing Ink you earn for first clears.

You start with a **Pen**. Every level win earns one **Eraser**, including replays. From Pages or the victory screen, open the **Pencil Case** to unlock the **Brush** for 2 Erasers or the **Highlighter** for 4. Unlocks and unused Erasers are saved in the same browser. Erasing a line in battle does not spend an Eraser.

The loadout and battle HUD show what you need to pass. On star pages, draw closed loops around the required board stars and keep the house standing through the final wave. Board stars are separate from the optional performance rating. The first time a monster or boss appears, the battle pauses for a short introduction. Select **Continue** to resume. Acknowledged introductions are remembered across levels and reloads.

**How to play** opens with a short animated example. The **Tool Guide** has replayable clips for each crayon, pen tip, seed packet, and drawing combo. You can open it in battle without advancing the wave.

Normal is the default and is meant to take real planning. Easy is gentler, Hard is tougher, and Nightmare unlocks after you clear the last page on Hard. Battery saver defaults to Auto. On Normal, the first two pages give you four hearts. Later pages give you three.

This is our own design. There are no soldiers, guns, or gore.
